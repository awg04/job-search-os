### "What is your biggest failure?"
**Reframe:** This was more of a technical challenge than a failure, but shows problem-solving and initiative.
**Story:** "At Carnival, I was building ETL pipelines to integrate third-party HVAC and energy management data into Power BI dashboards. The dedicated Azure engineer handling infrastructure didn't have domain knowledge of the third-party data dictionary — the structure and semantics of the incoming data feeds. The project stalled because we couldn't validate data quality or transformation logic.

Rather than wait, I took the initiative to learn Python, built notebooks to debug data transformations, and worked through data validation challenges with AI assistance. It meant stepping outside my typical BI-focused work, but it was worth it. I documented the data dictionary patterns for reuse and got the project back on course. The dashboards are now live and actively used by engineering teams for real-time troubleshooting.

What I learned: sometimes the fastest path forward is to build capability rather than wait for a bottleneck to resolve. And given the tools available now — AI-assisted coding, libraries like pandas and numpy — a BI engineer can credibly solve data engineering problems if the situation demands it."
